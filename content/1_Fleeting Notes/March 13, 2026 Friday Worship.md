The Cup of Sin vs. the Cup of Life: Which Will We Choose?
Exodus 32:20

As we live our lives, we inevitably face many moments of choice. Some choices become turning points in our lives, while others may seem insignificant. What always follows a choice is responsibility. Taking responsibility means facing the consequences of our decisions. However, many people try to make excuses or shift the blame to someone else when they make mistakes. Responsibility is the courage to accept the results of our actions and decisions. It is an essential part of growth. Avoiding responsibility may give us temporary comfort, but eventually the moment comes when we can no longer escape it. Why must we bear the consequences of our choices until the end?

When God created us, human beings were created in the image of God. In the Garden of Eden, humanity lived under God’s love. However, the whisper of the serpent touched the deep desires within the human heart. The desire to overthrow God’s order and become the master of one’s own life began to grow. Eve gave the fruit of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil to Adam, and Adam ate it immediately. In that moment, sin entered the world, and humanity confronted sin. Yet humanity avoided responsibility. When responsibility was avoided, suffering, labor, and death entered human life. The fall in the beginning was not simply disobedience. Yet although humanity fell, God prepared a way of restoration for us. Can we face our choices with our own strength? We must continually ask ourselves: What am I choosing right now? And what will I face at the end of that choice?

In today’s passage, Moses comes down from Mount Sinai and witnesses a shocking scene. He had intended to deliver the tablets given by God to the people with a joyful heart. However, the people had made a golden calf, worshiped it, and held a festival around it. When Moses saw this, he burned the golden calf, ground it into powder, scattered it on water, and made the people drink it. Why did he do that?

We cannot escape our choices. In the end, we must accept what we have made and confront what we have built. Living according to our own desires while ignoring responsibility and indulging ourselves is sin.

Why did Moses make the people drink it? This was not a simple incident. Through the powder that entered their bodies, God revealed that their very nature had been defiled. What they had worshiped as their god had now become dust, entered their bodies, and would eventually pass away as waste. This was God’s declaration that their sin was not light and that they must bear the cost of it. They were made to drink the consequence of their own sin. It clearly showed how sin is judged and what the cost of judgment is.

When we look at the Israelites drinking the powder of the golden calf, we must remember one person: Jesus. In the Garden of Gethsemane, Jesus prayed until His sweat became like drops of blood (Matthew 26:39). What was the cup that Jesus spoke about? What kind of cup was it that He desired to avoid? It was the result of our sin. That cup contained all of our disobedience, our sins, and our avoidance of responsibility (Isaiah 59:3–5). What He had to drink was not merely bitter water. It was the cup of judgment filled with God’s wrath against all the sins of humanity, something so filthy that anyone who drank it would surely die. Drop by drop, the weight of our sins filled that cup of judgment. It represented a complete curse. Yet Jesus drank the cup that we were supposed to drink. In order to love those who were unworthy of love, and to save those who could only face destruction because of sin, Jesus drank that cup completely.

We must understand this. Because Jesus drank the cup of judgment that we deserved, we no longer need to tremble in fear. Where God’s wrath should have fallen, the blood of Jesus was shed. All the suffering and judgment we should have carried were borne by Jesus. Do we truly know this love of Jesus, or do we simply ignore it? (Isaiah 51:22). The central message of this passage is that the cup of God’s wrath and judgment has now been taken away from us. God declares that the cup of punishment has been removed and that we will never drink it again. This promise has been fulfilled eternally through Jesus. By drinking the cup of wrath and judgment on our behalf, Jesus removed God’s cup of judgment from us. We must remember this grace and love.

Now the cup we drink is no longer the cup of judgment, but the cup of salvation filled with grace and life (Psalm 116:12–14). It is no longer the cup of wrath but the cup of salvation. Because Jesus drank the cup of judgment for us, we now lift up the cup of salvation. This passage proclaims that the blessing of eternal salvation has been given to us.

If we truly understand this grace of Jesus, how can we forget it? Jesus received the judgment that we deserved, and He poured out abundant life for us. We must break away from living thoughtlessly, simply going through life, eating, and doing only what is placed before us. Many people still have no interest in Jesus at all. The salvation Jesus has given us is the grace that came because He personally drank the cup of wrath. Humanity can never solve the problem of sin with its own power. It is a precious gift that we received because Jesus paid the full price for sin. Now all we must do is believe in Jesus and drink the cup of life, the cup of salvation.

When the Israelites made and worshiped the golden calf, God was filled with wrath, but He was also a God of love who gave them opportunity until the end. The ultimate result of that love is Jesus. By allowing Jesus to drink the cup of sin that we should have drunk, God fulfilled both His justice and His love. Therefore, the path is always open for those who repent. For believers, responsibility is a response to the grace we have received from God. It is not merely an obligation but a calling before God. Jesus bore all responsibility for us on the cross. This was not simply an expression of love, but an act done for us (Galatians 5:13). We must stop using the freedom God has given us as an opportunity to pursue worldly desires. That freedom is a privilege given to build the kingdom of God. For believers, responsibility means living out the life that God has spoken.

Who are we, and what path are we walking? Once we were people who wandered without knowing God. We longed for the visible things of the world and desired to enjoy what the world offered. At some point, we too made our own golden calves, clung to them, and sold our souls to those false idols, losing everything before them. We believed they would bring us happiness and peace, yet we lived miserable lives. Everything we loved more than God became our golden calf. But can those things truly save us? Do they give us true satisfaction? We were people who could only drink the consequence of sin. We were beings who had to bear the cost of our own sins.

Yet God did not leave us alone. God sent Jesus to drink that cup on our behalf. What does Jesus, who drank the cup of sin for us, truly mean to us? What does it mean to believe in Him? Do we understand what kind of cup Jesus faced on the cross in our place? That cup contained our despair and wounds, a cup so foul that no one could even bear its smell. Yet Jesus drank it for us. And on the cross He cried out, “It is finished.” He is our Savior.

We no longer need to drink the cup of sin. Now we only need to drink the cup of salvation and the cup of life. No matter how ugly our sins may be, God still calls us precious and honored. We are not people who walk the path of sin but those who walk the path of the cross.

The cup of sin or the cup of life. Which cup will we choose? We must hold onto and drink the cup of life, believing that Jesus is our Savior.

We are no longer people who look to the golden calf. Instead, let us look to the grace of the Lord that remains even in the brokenness of time (Isaiah 43:4). We are precious and holy people under the protection of the Lord. Because He has called us precious, we will never be forgotten. Let us fix our eyes on Jesus today and live holding firmly to the path of salvation that He accomplished.
